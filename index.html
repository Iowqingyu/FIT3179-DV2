01 — Before the shelf,
there's a journey.

The food on Australia's shelves has travelled further than you might think.
Where does it all come from? Explore the global connections behind Australia's sea-import flows and uncover the origins shaping each grocery department.

01 — From Global Shores to Local Stores

Food reaches Australian ports from around the world. Only its 15 heaviest recorded country-to-port (sea-imported) routes are shown. Thickness represents tonnes in the selected routes; curves are schematic, not vessel tracks.

SOURCE · National Freight Data Hub

02 — Australia's Food Imports by Department and Origin

Area encodes 2025 sea-import tonnes. Each department is
subdivided into leading origins plus Other.

SOURCE · National Freight Data Hub
THE INSIGHT

Pantry dominates import weight

Pantry and Fruit & vegetables together account for about two-thirds of recorded import tonnage. Import weight is concentrated in these departments.

KEY CONTEXT

Tonnage reflects shipment weight, not retail sales or the number of products.

03 — How Do Food Imports Reach Australia?

Two connected stages aggregate 2025 sea-import tonnage: food department → origin country → Australian arrival port. Other sources and ports are grouped.

SOURCE · National Freight Data Hub
THE INSIGHT

Two ports receive most of the volume

Sydney and Melbourne together receive roughly three-quarters of the mapped import tonnage. This concentration makes a few entry points especially important to the recorded supply network.

KEY CONTEXT

Port concentration may create exposure to disruption; the chart cannot measure actual supply resilience.

02 — So what actually
fills the shelves?

Moving from international trade to one retailer's recorded listings, we see a different perspective: product variety. The next visualisations describe the archived Coles catalogue (not purchases, demand, or sales).

04 — Which Department Dominates the Supermarket Aisles?

SOURCE · Australia Grocery Product Dataset
THE INSIGHT

Pantry accounts for most listings

Pantry represents about 58% of the archived listings, more than four times the next-largest department. The recorded assortment is heavily weighted toward shelf-stable goods, not evenly spread across aisles.

KEY CONTEXT

Listing counts show catalogue breadth, not how often products are purchased.

05 — Are Catalogue Listings Concentrated Among A Few Brands?

Ranked brand listing counts with a cumulative-percentage line; the percentage denominator is the 35 brands available in the supplied brand summary.


SOURCE · Australia Grocery Product Dataset
THE INSIGHT

One brand dominates this extract

Coles accounts for about 39% of listings among the 35 brands in this extract. The gap to the next brand points to substantial private-label breadth within this selected group.

KEY CONTEXT

This is a 35-brand extract, not the full brand market.

06 — A Different Shape of Australian Retail

The resized state geometries and sequential colour indicate its share of recorded listings in each state.

SOURCE · Australia Grocery Product Dataset + State Boundaries (GeoJSON)

The department mix changes little by state

Pantry accounts for roughly 57–60% of listings in every state and territory. This consistency suggests a broadly similar department-level catalogue mix despite geographic differences.

This compares the proportions within state listings.

03 — Does a special
offer depend on place?

Australia's supermarkets stretch across cities and regions, but the shopping experience is not necessarily the same everywhere. Beyond store locations, promotional patterns reveal how grocery offers differ across states.

07 — Coles and Woolworths Store Locations

Each dot represents one recorded Coles or Woolworths store at its reported coordinates.

Loading store-count map…

SOURCE · Coles and Woolworths Store-Location Snapshot
THE INSIGHT

Stores cluster near population centres

New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland contain about 78% of the mapped Coles and Woolworths locations. Store presence is geographically uneven, reflecting where the network is concentrated.

KEY CONTEXT

Store counts alone do not measure accessibility.

08 — Where Are Special Offers More Common?

Special offers are a familiar part of grocery shopping, but their recorded presence is not identical across Australia. Each state is shaded by its percentage of recorded catalogue listings flagged on special in that department.

SOURCE · Australia Grocery Product Dataset
THE INSIGHT

Produce specials vary most by state

Fruit & vegetables shows a wider state-to-state range in special-offer rates than most departments. In contrast, Pantry rates remain close to 27%, suggesting department-specific patterns matter more.

KEY CONTEXT

The rates describe promotion listings, not savings.

09 — Promotion Rates by State and Department

Promotions tell a more detailed story when geography and grocery departments meet. Some combinations show a greater share of discounted listings than others.


SOURCE · Australia Grocery Product Dataset
THE INSIGHT

Department matters more than location

Drinks has special-offer rates near 38–40% across states, while Bakery sits near 12–13%. That gap is much larger than most within-department state differences.

KEY CONTEXT

A high share of special offers does not show how large or valuable the discounts are.

04 — What about
the price tag?

Every grocery item comes with a price tag, but the story doesn't end at the checkout. As food prices change over time, so does the cost of filling Australia's grocery baskets.

10 — The Price of Filling Your Basket

From everyday pantry staples to meat and seafood, grocery package prices vary considerably. The horizontal price axis runs from A$0–40; higher outliers are outside this view.

SOURCE · Australia Grocery Product Dataset
THE INSIGHT

Meat sits at the higher end of package prices

Meat & seafood has a median listed price of A$10.50, compared with A$3.90 for fruit & vegetables. Drinks and Meat also show wider price spreads, so a single typical price hides substantial variation.

KEY CONTEXT

The dots show 105 sampled observations per department.

11 — Australia's Food Consumer Price Index (CPI) Trends

Grocery prices have not followed a single path over time. Australia's food categories have experienced different patterns of price growth, reshaping the cost of everyday essentials.

SOURCE · Australian Bureau of Statistics Monthly CPI Series
THE INSIGHT

Food categories do not rise in lockstep

Some food categories show steadier increases, while others fluctuate more sharply across the period. Even when the rate of increase slows, the index can remain well above its earlier level.

KEY CONTEXT

CPI tracks price change relative to an index base, not the dollar cost of a particular basket.

12 — How Have Food Prices Changed Over Time?

Annual ranking of year-on-year changes in average CPI, 2022–2025. As inflation changes from year to year, the ranking of food categories shifts with it.

SOURCE · Australian Bureau of Statistics Monthly CPI Series
THE INSIGHT

Relative pressure shifted by category

Vegetables ranked first in 2022 and 2024, but Fruit moved to first in 2025 at about 5.3% annual growth. Inflation pressure shifts between categories rather than following one fixed leader.

KEY CONTEXT

The ranks compare annual-average CPI growth rates, not absolute prices or household spending.
